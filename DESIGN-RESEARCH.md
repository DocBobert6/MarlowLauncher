# Marlow Launcher — Design Research (Consolidated)

**Project:** Marlow Launcher (`app.launcher`) — a Pixel-style Android home launcher, Jetpack Compose + Material 3.
**Date synthesised:** 2026-11-26.
**Status:** THE design document for the project. It replaces the six research tracks as the working reference; the tracks remain as source material in `~/workspace/launcher/design-research/` (tracks 01–06) and should be consulted when a rule below is disputed.
**Locked Marlow decisions this document applies** (project decisions, not research findings): default theme **Stock** (Material You one tap away); default accent **P22 phosphor blue** (`#3333CC` light / `#5555FF` dark); shading is the **warm CRT family** (light background `#F8F1E7`, dark `#262019`); glow is a **soft subtle halo, only on the page-indicator dot and the widget resize handle**; icon **C v3**.

**No credentials are claimed in this document.** It makes no claim about any author's employment, education, or qualifications, and no claim of having worked at any company or on any original product team. It stands on the public sources cited in §6. Where a claim here and a cited source disagree, the source wins — fix this document.

## 1. Purpose and method

This document synthesises six design-research tracks into one rulebook the Marlow rebuild is judged against:

| Track | Subject |
|---|---|
| 01 | Material Design 3 / Material You — the design system (m3.material.io, developer.android.com) |
| 02 | Stock launchers — Pixel Launcher as reference; Nova, Niagara, Lawnchair, Smart Launcher, Microsoft Launcher; Pixel history 2016–2026 |
| 03 | Home-screen design and iconography, plus an Apple HIG / iOS contrast study |
| 04 | Settings information architecture, onboarding / first run, gesture and motion, accessibility |
| 05 | AOSP Launcher3 source read (architecture, grid, drag and drop, folders, widgets, drawer, icons, badges, first run) |
| 06 | Google I/O / Android Dev Summit talks and design-engineering writing from people who built launchers |

**Method.** Every load-bearing claim rests on a cited public source that was actually read during the track research — the Material Design site, Android Developers documentation, AOSP source, Google Support, Apple HIG and Apple Support, launcher developers' own blogs / changelogs / interviews, or independent reviews and APK teardowns. Secondary sources (write-ups of a spec rather than the spec itself, forum mirrors of a changelog, teardown reporting) are flagged as such where they carry a rule. Where the tracks disagree, or a source is thin, beta-gated, or unverified, §3 says so inside the affected rule instead of silently picking a side. Where Material has no rule for something a launcher needs and a recommendation is an application of judgement, the rule is marked **[APP]**.

**How to use it.** §2 states the principles that govern Marlow. §3 is the consolidated, deduplicated rulebook — one numbered list, each rule checkable, each with its justifying source. §4 applies the rules to Marlow's locked decisions. §5 is the top 20 rules — the ones that will matter most when the rebuild is judged. §6 is the consolidated source list. §7 lists what the research could not settle.

**A note on "stock".** "Stock" in this document does **not** mean bare AOSP Launcher3. It means **Pixel Launcher as Google ships it on a Pixel** — Launcher3 plus Google's layers (At a Glance / Smartspace, Pixel Search, Discover, Material You theming, Quickstep recents). Copying AOSP alone would feel plainer than a Pixel; copying Pixel means copying its opinions, including its restrictions (track 02, §1). At the same time, the AOSP tree is *not* the Pixel Launcher: Pixel-only behaviour (At a Glance, the Google search bar, some prediction surfaces) lives in closed Google overlays, not in `platform/packages/apps/Launcher3` (track 05). Marlow copies AOSP mechanics and Pixel feel, and must not assume a Pixel-only feature exists in the AOSP source.

**The launcher is a host, not a showcase.** No single Google talk is *about* building a launcher; launcher guidance arrives sideways, in widget, gesture, Material You, and large-screen talks (track 06). The launcher is the only app that hosts other apps' widgets, icons, shortcuts, and wallpaper; it is the destination of back-to-home; and it is the surface gesture navigation starts from. Its quality is measured by how faithfully it renders other people's content.

## 2. Principles

Twelve principles govern Marlow. Each is stated plainly; the rules in §3 are the testable form of these principles.

1. **Roles, not values.** Nothing in the UI is a hard-coded hex. It is `primary`, `surface-container-high`, `on-surface-variant` — a role that resolves differently in light and dark, static and dynamic schemes, and at different contrast levels. If a value appears in more than one place, it belongs in the theme. (Track 01, §§1–2; track 06, R14.)
2. **Every colour is a tone; contrast is a property of the derivation, not a check at the end.** A scheme is five key colours expanded into tonal palettes of 13 tones each; roles are fixed tones from those palettes. A tone difference of 40 guarantees contrast ≥ 3.0 and 50 guarantees ≥ 4.5 — so picking two tones far enough apart *is* the contrast check. If a pairing breaks, fix the derivation (seed/variant), never a one-off hex tweak. (Track 01, §§1, 2.2, Part IV §2.)
3. **Depth is tonal, not shadow.** M3 communicates elevation primarily through surface colour (a primary-coloured tint deepening with height); shadows are used only where extra separation is needed. A launcher full of drop shadows looks like Material 2, and in dark themes shadows are invisible — the tint *is* the depth cue. (Track 01, §§1, 4.)
4. **Motion is choreography, not decoration.** Every transition answers "where did this come from, and where is it going?" Entering elements decelerate; exiting elements accelerate; bigger travel takes longer; exits are faster than enters. Drags, flings, and sheet settling use springs; screen transitions use the easing table. Never mix the two jobs. (Track 01, §5; track 04, §§3.3, 8.2.)
5. **State is a layer, not a new colour.** Interaction feedback is a translucent overlay of the content's own colour at a fixed opacity per state — hover 8%, focus 10%, press 10%, drag 16%, disabled 38% — with a 40dp layer inside a 48dp target, and a touch-point ripple on press. Selection is a container change, never opacity alone. (Track 01, §6.)
6. **The home screen is a glance-and-launch surface, not a dashboard.** Three jobs, in order: answer "what do I need to know right now?" without opening an app; launch the handful of daily apps in one tap; get out of the way. Every item on page one must earn its place by being seen, launched, or acted on immediately; anything else belongs in a folder, on a secondary page, or in All Apps. (Track 03, §1.1.)
7. **Visual hierarchy: one thing first, one thing second, everything else quiet.** The glanceable strip at the top, the dock at the bottom, the grid as a calm even field in the middle. Space does most of the work — whitespace is the tool that creates focus, not empty space left over after placing things. The grid itself has no boxes, dividers, or per-icon backgrounds. (Track 03, §§1.2–1.3.)
8. **The stock feeling is restraint.** Pixel's appeal is that it is boring and reliable: nothing shouts, nothing is half-finished, nothing asks the user to make a decision before they have launched a single app. The stock feeling is a small number of strong opinions, applied consistently, with nothing half-finished visible. (Track 02, §1; track 03, §1.6.)
9. **Icons are a system, not a collection.** One mask, one apparent size, one corner-radius logic, one colour discipline — for every icon on the device. Android's adaptive-icon system exists to enforce that uniformity across apps the launcher author never drew; the launcher is the party that applies the mask. (Track 03, §§1.5, 3.1–3.2; track 05, §9.)
10. **The wallpaper is the background, never a competitor.** Labels and icons sit on a background the launcher does not control. Contrast is judged against the *worst* pixel behind the text, not the average, and is guaranteed by construction — a scrim or a surface container — never by hoping the wallpaper is dark. (Track 03, §§1.4, 4.1–4.2; track 01, §1.6.)
11. **Settings are few, plainly worded, and show their values; the first run asks one question.** Pixel's own settings surface is tiny (Wallpaper & style / Home settings / Widgets). A default must be what most users would choose; every permission is asked in context, at first use, never up front; a declined permission disables one feature, never the app. (Track 04, §§1–2; track 02, §1.10.)
12. **Marlow does not own Home or Recents — and must not pretend to.** Quickstep gesture navigation and the Recents provider role are reserved to the stock launcher; a third-party launcher cannot reproduce them. The stock-feeling move is to coexist cleanly: never fight the system gesture, hand off app-open/close animations cleanly, and invest the effort in the workspace, drawer, and drag feel instead. (Track 02, §1.11; track 05, §8; track 04, §3.5; track 06, §8.2.)

## 3. The consolidated rulebook

*How to read it: one numbered list, deduplicated across the six tracks. Each rule states a pass/fail test and names the source that justifies it. **[APP]** marks a recommendation that is an application of judgement where Material (or the platform) has no rule for the launcher case — it is labelled so it can be challenged without disturbing the sourced rules. Where tracks disagree or a source is uncertain, the disagreement is stated inside the rule. Track cross-references (e.g. "T1 R8") let a reader trace each rule back to its track of origin.*

### A. Colour and theme

**1. No raw hex colour appears in any composable outside the theme definition.** Check: search UI code for `Color(0xFF…)` outside `Theme.kt` / `Color.kt`. Source: m3.material.io, Colour roles (roles are tokenized); developer.android.com, Material Design 3 in Compose. (T1 R1; T6 R14.)

**2. Every text/icon colour is the `on-` role of the background it sits on** — `primary`↔`on-primary`, `primary-container`↔`on-primary-container`, `secondary-container`↔`on-secondary-container`, `surface*`↔`on-surface`/`on-surface-variant`, `error-container`↔`on-error-container`. No cross-pairing (never `on-surface` text on `primary-container`). Check: list every (background, foreground) pair per screen. Source: m3.material.io, Colour roles ("apply colors only in the intended pairs or layering orders… Combining colors improperly may break contrast"); developer.android.com, Material Design 3 in Compose — Accessibility (the ❌ `tertiaryContainer` + `primaryContainer` example). (T1 R2.)

**3. Dynamic colour is on by default on Android 12+, and the app falls back to the static Stock scheme below API 31 and when dynamic colour is off.** Check: colourful wallpaper on API 31+ re-themes the launcher; dynamic colour disabled shows the Stock scheme, not a default purple. Source: developer.android.com, Material Design 3 in Compose (`dynamicLightColorScheme` / `dynamicDarkColorScheme` on SDK ≥ S, custom static `ColorScheme` fallback); m3.material.io, Dynamic colour overview. (T1 R3.)

**4. The Stock scheme is generated from a single seed through the HCT pipeline, with light and dark schemes exported — no role hand-tuned afterwards.** The seed is the decided P22 phosphor blue; the warm light/dark backgrounds are the neutral palette's expression and must land on `surface` / `surface-container-*` roles so every surface inherits the warmth consistently. If a role looks wrong, the seed is wrong. Check: the theme file is an export (or faithful transcription) from one seed via Material Theme Builder or `material-color-utilities`; changing the seed regenerates everything. Source: developer.android.com, Material Design 3 in Compose ("Generate color schemes… The Material Theme Builder tool allows you to do this"); m3.material.io, Colour roles (roles map to fixed tones, differently in light and dark — never just invert a light scheme). (T1 R4, §1.5; T6, Part A 2021 Material You.)

**5. Dynamic colour is the same pipeline with a different seed.** When the user enables Material You, the wallpaper's extracted colour replaces the P22 seed and the whole scheme regenerates. The app must not contain a second, parallel set of colours for dynamic mode — it swaps the *scheme*, not individual colours. Contrast levels (standard/medium/high) are the user's, not the app's; never hard-code one, and never "fix" a pairing by darkening text manually. Source: m3.material.io, Dynamic colour overview; Colour roles (three contrast levels); developer.android.com, Material Design 3 in Compose. (T1 §1.5.)

**6. Surface-container steps are respected: use container roles more than one step apart.** The dock uses `surface-container` and the search bar uses `surface-container-high`; the search bar must be visibly distinct from the surface behind it. Check: screenshot the home screen; hierarchy must still read with shadows mentally removed. Source: m3.material.io, Search guidelines ("use surface container roles that are more than one step apart"); Colour roles (surface-container component mapping). (T1 R5, R22.)

**7. Body text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 and non-text/icon/boundary contrast ≥ 3:1, in both light and dark schemes, at standard, medium, and high contrast levels.** Check: measure with a contrast checker against the actual resolved colours, not the seed. Source: developer.android.com, Accessibility (4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large text and non-text); m3.material.io, Colour roles (role pairs provide ≥ 3:1 by construction); WCAG via the colour-contrast codelab. (T1 R7.)

**8. Error colour is used only for errors/destructive states, nowhere decorative; the phosphor halo is an additive effect on top of role-coloured elements, never the sole carrier of state or meaning, and never a substitute for a role.** Check: search for `error` role usage; turn the halo off — every state must still read. Source: m3.material.io, Colour roles (error roles are static by default; never recoloured for decoration); project decision for the halo. **[APP]** for the halo half — M3 has no halo rule; the "never the sole carrier" constraint is the application of M3's state-layer and colour-role rules. (T1 R9, R10.)

### B. Typography, labels, and legibility over wallpaper

**9. All text uses a type-scale role (`MaterialTheme.typography.*`); no hard-coded `fontSize` at call sites; all text sizes are in sp so the user's font-size setting works.** Check: search for `.sp` literals outside the theme; enable the system's largest font size — the UI must scale without clipping or overlap. Source: developer.android.com, Material Design 3 in Compose — Typography; Accessibility ("specify font size in scalable pixels (sp)"); m3.material.io, Typography overview (15 baseline + 15 emphasized styles). (T1 R11, R14; T4 A5.)

**10. App-icon labels (home, dock, drawer, folders) are 12sp — `body-small` or `label-medium` — one role, used everywhere, single line, ellipsis.** No text below 12sp anywhere in the app (11sp is the floor for a label only). Check: measure label size in screenshots across all four surfaces. Source: developer.android.com, Accessibility (12sp body floor); M3 type scale (`body-small` 12/16, `label-medium` 12/16). **[APP]** — Material has no component spec for home-screen app-icon labels; 12sp in one of the two 12sp roles is the honest application of the scale. (T1 R12, R13; T3 R37.)

**11. Labels are one line, truncated with an ellipsis — never two lines, never clipped mid-glyph.** *Disagreement/uncertainty flagged:* track 02 reports (from an Android 15 DP2 teardown, not Google-documented) that Pixel trims names longer than ~9 characters and was considering a "Show long app names" two-line option. The tracks agree on one line + ellipsis as the stock rule; the two-line option is reported direction only, not a settled Pixel behaviour. Marlow picks one line + ellipsis everywhere (home, drawer, folders) until the device evidence says otherwise. Check: a 20+-character app name in all three places. Source: T1 §2.3; T3 R38; T2 §1.9. (T1 R12; T2 rule 20; T3 R38.)

**12. Every cell has the same height — icon + fixed label slot — whether the label is short, long, truncated, or hidden.** Labels survive 200% font scaling without clipping or overlapping: at 200% a label truncates, never overlaps the icon below it. Hiding labels (an explicit, whole-screen, opt-in setting only) must not change cell height — the label slot becomes breathing room. Check: font scale 200% on device and in preview. Source: m3.material.io, Grids & spacing; developer.android.com, Accessibility; WCAG resize-text expectation (200%, Android 14 non-linear scaling). (T3 R39, R40, R41; T4 A6; T1 §2.3.)

**13. Labels meet 4.5:1 against the worst patch of wallpaper behind them, not the average.** The "background" is the lightest pixel in the label's band (for light labels) on each of four test wallpapers: bright photo, dark photo, busy photo, flat colour. Check: measure with a contrast checker on all four. Source: developer.android.com, Accessibility (4.5:1 normal text); T3 §4.1 (worst-pixel rule; Willow Launcher measurements: a fixed 36% scrim read as a black column on dark wallpaper, a 22% dim left the clock at 2.96:1 on pale wallpaper, a luminance-aware dim put the clock at 4.99:1 and the date at 6.51:1 — the numbers, not the intention, are the test). (T1 R8; T3 R48; T4 A3.)

**14. Text over wallpaper is legible by construction: a scrim or a surface container behind it, never shadow alone.** Flat scrim 30–50% black under white text; gradient scrim transparent at one end, 50–70% black where the text sits, eased with intermediate stops so there is no visible band. Scrim strength follows the wallpaper's brightness (strong over bright art, fading to nothing over dark art, ~8% veil as fallback when no wallpaper pixels can be read); a fixed-strength scrim on all wallpapers fails, and no scrim edge, seam, or shoulder may be visible. Check: white wallpaper, black wallpaper, busy photo — read every label; smooth light wallpaper shows no scrim line. Source: m3.material.io, Colour roles (scrim role); T3 §4.2; T6 R17. **[APP]** for the wallpaper case — the scrim mechanism is M3's; applying it to icon labels is the launcher's application. (T1 R8; T3 R49, R50; T4 A4.)

### C. Shape, elevation, state layers

**15. Shape discipline: buttons, chips, badges, and nav indicators are `full` (pill); cards are `medium` (12dp); dialogs and expanded bottom sheets are `extra-large` (28dp); text fields, menus, and snackbars are `extra-small` (4dp); FAB is `large` (16dp).** No large/full corners on information-dense components. The shape scale is not redefined for brand purposes; any custom radius is a recorded, deliberate override of a single component. Check: measure corner radii in screenshots against this table; the theme's `Shapes` match M3 defaults (4/8/12/16/28) except for listed overrides. Source: m3.material.io, Shape (corner radius scale; "By default, buttons are mapped to the full corner radius shape style"); developer.android.com, Material Design 3 in Compose — Shapes. (T1 R18, R20, R21.)

**16. Nested rounded containers use optical roundness (inner radius = outer radius − padding), never the same radius twice.** Check: folder previews, widget frames inside cards, search bar inside a sheet. Source: m3.material.io, Shape ("Use different corner radii values for nested components"). (T1 R19.)

**17. Depth is expressed by surface-container roles, not shadows: home = `surface`, dock = `surface-container`, search bar = `surface-container-high`, sheet/dialog = `surface-container-high`, dragged icon = highest level.** In dark theme depth still reads (tonal tint, not shadow). No component's default elevation is changed without a recorded reason; press/hover raises elevation by exactly one level (FAB 3→4 on hover). Check: same hierarchy screenshots in light and dark. Source: m3.material.io, Elevation ("Avoid changing the default elevation of Material components"; "M3: Using color instead of shadows to communicate elevation"); developer.android.com, Elevation (dark-theme tonal overlays). (T1 R22–R25.)

**18. Modal sheets and dialogs sit over a 32% black scrim; the home screen is never scrimmed except behind a modal surface.** Bottom sheets: top corners 28dp, bottom square, drag handle present (32×4dp pill in `on-surface-variant` at 40% opacity); modal sheets block interaction, standard sheets don't. Dialogs: `surface-container-high`, 28dp corners, 24dp side insets, headline `titleLarge`, supporting text `bodyMedium`, text-button actions, used sparingly and only for blocking decisions. Check: every sheet and dialog in the app. Source: m3.material.io, Bottom sheets guidelines; Dialogs guidelines; Dialog/BottomSheet scrim attributes (32%). (T1 R26, R48, R49.)

**19. Every pressable element shows press feedback: a state layer at 10% of the content colour and/or a touch-point ripple — no dead taps anywhere, including app icons, dock icons, drawer rows, settings rows, and chips.** Hover 8% and focus 10% layers exist and are distinguishable from press; dragged elements show the 16% drag layer; disabled elements read as disabled (38% treatment). The state layer is 40dp and the interactive target is 48dp — the feedback is smaller than the hit area, never larger. Selection is shown by a container/indicator change (e.g. `secondary-container` pill, filled icon), never by opacity alone. Check: press, hover (mouse/trackpad), and keyboard/d-pad focus every element type. Source: m3.material.io, State layers (opacities read directly from the spec page; "The size of state layers is 40dp while the interactive target size is 48dp"); developer.android.com, Material Design 3 in Compose (ripple starts at the touch point; overscroll is a stretch effect, not the old edge glow). (T1 R34–R39.)

### D. Home screen, grid, dock, glance strip

**20. First boot is not empty: on a fresh install the first frame is a complete home screen** — glanceable strip with real date + weather, at least 8 app/folder cells on page one, a 5-icon dock, and a present, tappable search entry point. No wizard, blank grid, setup card, tips card, settings shortcut, or store shortcut is visible in the first frame. Check: cold-install, clear data, open Marlow, screenshot the first frame and compare side-by-side with a Pixel first-boot screenshot. Source: T2 §1.10, rules 1, 37; T3 R1; T6 §6.1. **[APP]** in part — no Google-published "first-boot must contain exactly N apps" specification exists; the counts are the synthesised Marlow default to test, not a platform mandate (T3 §2.1). (T2 rule 1; T3 R1, R22.)

**21. Page one holds 8–12 app/folder cells plus the dock, with visible empty space.** At least one full grid row (or its equivalent) on page one is empty; a completely full first page reads as a shelf. At most one widget on first boot besides the glanceable strip, and it must not duplicate information already in the strip. No second page of apps the user never asked for, no empty page. Check: count cells at default settings. Source: T3 §2.1, R4, R11, R21; T2 §6.1. **[APP]** — counts synthesised from Pixel conventions and the functional-layer model (Glance / Action / Stable-access layers). (T3 R4, R11, R21.)

**22. Grid options are exactly the Pixel set (2×2, 3×3, 4×4, 4×5, 5×5), default 5×5 on a modern Pixel-size phone (4×5 on smaller phones).** *Disagreement flagged:* AOSP Launcher3's grid-options are `3_by_3 / 4_by_4 / 5_by_5 / 6_by_5 / desktop_6_by_5` (tablet `6_by_5` added 2021), each with its own DB file and default layout — the Pixel user-facing picker (2×2–5×5) is the same mechanism surfaced in Settings, not the full AOSP set. Marlow follows the Pixel set. Changing grid re-flows icons without overlap or loss, migrates and drops overflow (telling the user), and never silently reflows; grid choice is made once, at DB creation, from the closest display-option, then persisted. Check: switch 5×5 → 4×4; surviving items keep relative positions. Source: 9to5Google Pixel setup guide (five options, 5×5 recommended); `res/xml/device_profiles.xml` + `InvariantDeviceProfile.java`; T5 rules 2, 47, 48. (T2 rule 2; T5 rules 1, 2, 47, 48.)

**23. The grid is a data record, not a layout file.** Workspace columns/rows, folder rows/cols, hotseat count, all-apps columns, and icon sizes come from a single grid-option record (name + rows/columns/folder grid/hotseat count/drawer columns/icon size/DB file/default layout); changing the record changes all of them. Cell size is derived, never hard-coded: (available width/height − padding − border space) ÷ column/row count. Horizontal margin is capped at 14% of screen width. One occupancy table (`boolean[][]`) per page; no two items overlap and a widget span is an all-or-nothing rectangle. Check: rotate to landscape — cell size recomputes, icons do not clip or overlap; place a 2×2 widget, then attempt to drop an icon on any of its four cells — all four reject. Source: `DeviceProfile.java` (`MAX_HORIZONTAL_PADDING_PERCENT = 0.14f`, `calculateCellWidth/Height`); `CellLayout.java` (`GridOccupancy`); `InvariantDeviceProfile.java`. (T5 rules 1, 3, 4, 5.)

**24. Icons, labels, and gutters are identical on home, drawer, folders, dock, and search results** — same dp size, same label size, same spacing, measured in screenshots, not eyeballed. One gap token everywhere (8dp scale, `space100 = 8dp` base); the icon-to-label gap is a single smaller token, identical for every cell; edge padding (medium/large) is visibly larger than the inter-cell gap; spacing is defined on the parent container, not as per-child margins. Check: screenshot all five surfaces. Source: m3.material.io, Spacing (padding/gaps on the parent; margins a last resort); T3 §4.3, R51–R54; T2 §6.2 rule 1. (T2 rule 5; T3 R51–R54.)

**25. The dock holds exactly 5 icons, labels off, one row, one page, present on every home page.** Dock icon size equals grid icon size (100–110%). A 6th icon cannot be added (refused with feedback, not wrapped or silently dropped). The dock is stable across pages — no suggested or recent apps in it — and is separated from the grid by visibly more than one row gap. No drawer button in the dock. *Note:* track 01 states dock labels "follow the same rule" as grid labels; tracks 02/03 record the Pixel convention as labels off in the dock (muscle memory for the five most familiar apps). Marlow follows the Pixel convention (labels off in the dock) and records this as a deliberate deviation from track 01's generalisation. Check: swipe to any page — same five icons, same order. Source: Pixel convention via TechWiser / XDA Pixel-look guide; T3 §2.2, R6, R8, R9, R53; T2 §1.4, rule 3. (T2 rule 3; T3 R6, R8, R9.)

**26. Dock contents are the daily five by kind: Phone, Messages (or the user's messenger), browser, mail, Camera.** *Gap flagged, not settled:* Google publishes no canonical first-boot dock list; screenshot evidence commonly shows Phone / Messages / Chrome / Photos / Camera, and one Launcher3-fork README claims six items (inconsistent with the 5-icon dock — treat with suspicion). Marlow's dock is Marlow's curated choice by kind, labelled as such — do not present any list as "the Pixel default". Check: fresh install shows one calling/messaging pair, one browser, one mail, one camera; no game, social, or store app in the default dock. Source: T2 §1.4 (gap stated); T3 R7. (T2 rule — gap; T3 R7.)

**27. At a Glance sits at the top of the main home page only, with no card background or border — text directly on the wallpaper.** Resting state shows day + date and weather (condition + temperature); tapping weather opens weather, tapping date opens Calendar. An upcoming calendar event *replaces* the date line (it does not appear as a second row or a badge). It is a carousel, not a static widget: swiping cycles complications (today/tomorrow weather, events, reminders, etc.) and the content changes with time/context. Long-press offers Settings (per-feature toggles) and an explanation of where its content comes from; turning features off reduces it to day/date — never a blank or an error. It must never show a news feed, a persistent greeting, a promotional card, more than one event at a time, a full agenda, or a battery percentage. Check: each behaviour in turn. Source: 9to5Google Pixel setup; support.google.com At a Glance; PhoneArena complications list; T3 §2.4. (T2 rules 8–12; T3 R2, R3.)

**28. One search entry point on the home screen, thumb-reachable at the bottom, below the dock on every home page** — pill-shaped, with the leading mark on the left and mic/Lens on the right. *Disagreement flagged:* M3's search guidance places a search bar "at the top of a screen to remain prominent"; the Pixel convention is the bottom (thumb reach, above the gesture handle). Marlow follows the Pixel bottom placement as a deliberate, documented deviation for ergonomics, and the bar must still be persistent and prominent. The dock pill and the drawer-top search are *different* surfaces with defined jobs (see rule 33) — do not make both open the same screen silently. Check: exactly one home search entry point; tapping the pill opens search immediately with no blank screen and no second tap. Source: m3.material.io, Search guidelines (placement, `surface-container-high`, ≤2 trailing icons, hint text, full-screen focused search on compact); T2 §1.5, rules 4, 13; T3 R10. (T1 R47; T2 rules 4, 13; T3 R10.)

**29. Wallpaper parallaxes with page scroll; page swipes follow the finger, snap on release, and never scroll past the last page with a dead bounce.** Swipe between home pages uses the Simple or None scroll effect — no cube, card-stack, zoom, or bounce transition at default settings. Empty trailing pages are removed (snap-off 400 ms, fade 150 ms); dragging to the edge creates one. Check: drag a page halfway and release (returns), fling (advances one page), swipe past the last page (short overshoot, settles, no blank space). Long-pressing the icon reveals the notifications (and app shortcuts). ### H. Motion, gestures, haptics, back

**53. Screen transitions use the easing table; exits are faster than enters; duration scales with travel distance.** Enter = Emphasized decelerate 400 ms; exit (permanent) = Emphasized accelerate 200 ms; begin-and-end-on-screen = Emphasized 500 ms; small utility transitions = Standard 300/250/200 ms. No single-step transition exceeds 500 ms; no micro-interaction (press ripple, toggle, icon-state change) exceeds 200 ms. (T1 R27–R29; T4 G7–G9.)

**54. The right transition pattern is used for each transition.** Container transform for one-thing-becomes-another (folder icon → open folder; search bar → search view; widget → widget config); shared axis for navigational steps (home → drawer on the z-axis; settings forward/back on the x-axis; drawer pages); fade-through for unrelated content swaps (At a Glance content changing; switching unrelated sections — outgoing fades out, incoming fades in while scaling 92% → 100%); fade for in-bounds appear/disappear (dialogs, menus, tooltips, badges — entering scales 80% → 100%). Transitions run as pairs (exit + enter together) so direction reads; choreography is container first, contents stagger 30–60 ms after. Check: name the pattern for each transition in the app. Source: developer.android.com, Material motion codelab ("Building Beautiful Transitions with Material Motion"); m3.material.io, Easing and duration. (T1 R30; T4 G6, G10.)

**55. Drags, flings, and sheet settling use springs (M3 Expressive motion), not fixed-duration tweens; screen transitions use the easing table, not springs.** At most two "hero" motion moments in the whole launcher (e.g. folder open, first-run welcome) — no other screen has a hero animation. Only transform and opacity are animated — never width/height/top/left/margin/padding — and never animate font size (animate bitmaps/bounds/opacity instead). Check: drag a sheet/icon and feel for velocity-responsive settling; code-review the motion table. (T1 R31; T4 X7, X8; T6 Part A 2025 Expressive.)

**56. Long-press fires at the system timeout (500 ms default), read from the system, not hard-coded, and respects the user's Accessibility long-press timeout.** Haptic fires *at* the long-press moment, not on lift; the icon does not move before it, and drag starts only after that haptic. Drag starts from long-press; the drag visual is scaled up; the source cell shows a hole/placeholder, not a duplicate. Dragging shrinks the workspace (spring-loaded mode, `SPRING_LOADED` state) so more pages and drop targets are visible. Reorder preview is live but provisional and commits only after a 650 ms hover (`REORDER_TIMEOUT = 650` in current AOSP; 350 ms in older revisions — Marlow uses 650 ms and notes the older value as historical). Hover < 650 ms and move away — nothing moves; hold ≥ 650 ms — neighbours slide aside (temporary coordinates; real layout untouched until drop). Dragging to the screen edge auto-scrolls pages and can create a new page; dragging to the top reveals Remove/Uninstall. Check: each timing with a stopwatch; grep for hard-coded 300/400/800 ms long-press thresholds (none may exist). Source: `ViewConfiguration.getLongPressTimeout()` (LONG_PRESS_TIMEOUT = 500; TAP_TIMEOUT 115 ms; DOUBLE_TAP_TIMEOUT 300 ms); `Workspace.java` (`REORDER_TIMEOUT`, `beginDragShared`); `DragController.java` (spring-loaded mode). (T4 G1–G3; T5 rules 9–11, 15.)

**57. Marlow does not intercept or re-implement the system swipe-up-and-hold (Overview/Recents) gesture, and does not provide system recents.** With Marlow as default on a gesture-nav device, swipe-up from an app still reaches the *system* recents, and Marlow shows no second, competing recents UI. The swipe-home animation is icon-anchored where the platform allows it, and the team honestly records on the Pixel 10a whether it is ("icon-anchored" or "not icon-anchored — QuickStep boundary"), with no claim of a fix that was not observed. App launch: the icon scales / the window grows from the icon; it never crossfades from nowhere. The launcher does not layer its own animation on top of system transitions (app open/close, overview, predictive back). Check: on the Pixel 10a, swipe home from an app and watch the window. Source: `quickstep/` (`TouchInteractionService`, `AbsSwipeUpHandler`, `RecentsView`) — system-privileged role; XDA, "Here's why Nova Launcher works better on Google Pixel phones" (QuickStep boundary); Esper (Rahman, with Kevin Barry) on the predictive-back prototype. (T5 rule 36; T4 X1, X2, G21; T1 R33; T6 §8.2.)

**58. Predictive back is supported everywhere through the ahead-of-time back model; at the home-screen root, back belongs to the system.** Every overlay (drawer, folder, widget picker, long-press menu, settings sub-screens) declares back handling via `BackHandler` / `PredictiveBackHandler` with state-driven `enabled` (one callback per responsibility; `enabled` follows observable UI state, not a conditional checked after the event; no callback runs business logic or logging); no screen uses `KeyEvent.KEYCODE_BACK` or `onBackPressed`. A back swipe on a full-screen launcher surface previews the destination (scale to ~90%, fade-through at 35% progress); cancelling restores it with no state change. No Marlow edge gesture competes with system Back; a drawer inside Marlow uses the peek pattern (tap-and-hold the edge), not a competing edge swipe. Gesture-exclusion rects are narrow and justified — only a rect that is genuinely a launcher drag handle — and the home gesture is never excluded. At the home root with no overlay open, a back gesture plays the back-to-home system animation; Marlow does not intercept it, open a menu, or do nothing. Check: open each overlay and start a back swipe — a preview animates; grep for the deprecated back APIs. (T4 G12, X3; T6 R20–R23.)

**59. Haptics use predefined constants, never raw `vibrate(long)`; less is more.** Haptics fire once, crisply, at the event moment for: long-press pickup, drag snap, drop, page snap, folder/drawer threshold, toggle flip, successful confirm. No haptic for: icon tap, page swipe, scrolling, typing in search, opening an app, or errors (visual only). Repeated haptics during a drag are discrete, quiet ticks with silence between them — never one long buzz. Keyclick-scale feedback is 10–20 ms (predefined CLICK/TICK class). Haptics and visuals land on the same frame. On Android 16+, any custom haptic is an envelope (PWLE — `BasicEnvelopeBuilder` / `WaveformEnvelopeBuilder`), not a one-shot, with a predefined-constant fallback. Marlow never overrides the system haptics setting (it may offer its own on/off defaulting to "follow system" — never "force on"). Haptic review is done by hand on the Pixel 10a, naming each effect click / tick / low tick. Check: perform each event and note haptics; grep for `Vibrator.vibrate(`. Source: developer.android.com, Haptics design principles ("less is more… Avoid using the legacy one-shot vibrations"); source.android.com, Haptics UX foundation (clear/rich/buzzy; crisp discrete ticks); blog.google, "Haptic design: How Google builds and designs products to feel just right" (Hong Tan / Debanjan Mukherjee: click, tick, low tick, thud, quick fall; the 200–300 Hz "soprano" band avoided); World Haptics Conference 2025 tutorial (Khalil, Kulczak & Tan; PWLE APIs). (T4 G13–G20, X9, X10.)

**60. Reduced motion is honoured, and nothing flashes more than 3 times per second.** When the user has animations off (Settings → Accessibility → Colour and motion → Remove animations, or the three animation scales set to Off), slides/zooms/parallax are replaced by crossfades or instant changes, with no large translate/scale motion remaining; opacity-only changes are kept, and essential feedback (a progress indicator) is kept. Check: enable "Remove animations" and repeat rules 53–55. Source: platform accessibility guidance; WCAG 2.3.1 (photosensitive epilepsy). (T1 R32; T4 A13, A14.)

### I. Settings, onboarding, permissions

**61. Top level has 5–7 sections, not 10+; no single screen shows more than 15 rows.** Every sub-screen title matches its entry label exactly. No vague section titles ("General", "Misc", "Other settings") as a top-level title. "Advanced" only when ≥3 items are hidden, and its subtext lists them on one line, truncated with an ellipsis. The entry point is exactly "Settings" (long-press menu: "Home settings") and nowhere else as a synonym — no "Options", "Preferences", or "Launcher options". Check: count rows on every settings screen. Source: source.android.com, Settings guidelines ("Showing more than 10-15 items can be overwhelming"; "Avoid using vague titles like 'General settings.'"); developer.android.com, Settings pattern (label matches subscreen title; "Don't use synonyms such as 'Options' or 'Preferences.'"). (T4 S1–S4, S9, S10.)

**62. Every row shows its current value as supporting text, and wording follows the official rules.** Subtext states the value ("5 × 4", "On", "System default"), never a description of the setting. Labels are brief, meaningful, most-important-word-first; no generic-verb labels (Set / Change / Edit / Modify / Manage / Use / Select / Choose); no row repeats the section title's words; no negative labels ("Don't", "Never" — rephrase neutrally, e.g. "Block"); no first-person labels ("Notify me" → "Notifications"); impersonal labels, sentence case everywhere; buttons are Verb + Object ("Reset home screen", not "Reset" alone). No user-visible string contains a plan/feature number, a ticket number, or a developer aside. Check: read every settings string; grep labels. Source: developer.android.com, Settings pattern; source.android.com, Settings guidelines; Material writing convention (sentence case). (T4 S5–S8, S24; T6 R16; T3 R55.)

**63. Setting, status, and action are three different rows and look different.** A setting has a control or a chevron; a read-only status line (e.g. "Home app: Marlow") has no switch, no chevron, no ripple that implies it is tappable, and lives at the bottom of a screen or on an About screen — never mixed into the middle of toggles. An action (Reset layout, Back up, Restore, Clear search history) is a one-off verb with no on/off state; destructive actions are separated, grey/negative, and confirmed. App info (version, licences, storage) is status lines on its own About screen, not toggles and not between preference rows. A dependent setting sits directly below its parent, disabled with a reason ("Turn on Suggestions to change this") rather than disappearing; a feature that can be turned off wholesale has one master switch at the top of its own screen. Check: every screen, every row type. Source: developer.android.com, Settings pattern ("Avoid information about the app, such as a version number"; dependency pattern); source.android.com, Settings guidelines (title + status pattern; main-setting pattern). (T4 S13–S16.)

**64. Controls follow the platform patterns: switches over checkboxes; single-choice settings use radio on a sub-screen/dialog, never radio rows in a list; a single on/off uses a switch, never a radio pair.** Switches: selected = `primary`, unselected track = `surface-container-highest` + `outline`; sliders: active track/thumb = `primary`, inactive track = `surface-container-highest`, value shown; both have 48dp targets. Toggles apply immediately — there is no Save button in Settings (a choice that needs Apply, e.g. grid size with a preview, is the documented exception and its button says "Apply"). Frequently used actions (add widget, change wallpaper, rename a folder, remove an app from home) live in context by long-press on the thing itself, not in Settings. No device-level preference is duplicated (no Marlow toggle for system dark-theme scheduling, system font size, system animation scale, or system notification sound). Check: count checkboxes (zero, except a documented genuine negative-action list); flip a toggle, leave, return — the change persisted. Source: source.android.com, Settings guidelines ("Use switches over checkboxes"; "Radio buttons should not be used in settings list"); developer.android.com, Settings pattern ("Don't include frequently accessed actions"; "Avoid replicating preferences available at the device settings level"); M3 Switch/Slider tokens. (T4 S11, S12, S17–S19; T1 R51.)

**65. Search over settings exists, is live, and deep-links.** For 15+ settings, results filter as you type, match title, synonyms, and effect words ("dark" → dark theme), each result shows its breadcrumb path ("Home settings › Drawer"), tapping a result lands directly on that setting's sub-screen with the row visible/highlighted, and a no-match query shows an honest empty state ("No settings match '…'"), not a blank list. Settings is reachable in ≤2 taps from the home screen (long-press empty space → "Home settings") and from the drawer. Check: type "grid" / "dots" / "dark". Source: developer.android.com, Settings pattern ("For 15 or more settings … add search functionality"); source.android.com, Settings guidelines (results show title, subtext, breadcrumb). (T4 S20, S21; T6 R29.)

**66. First run: the home screen appears before any question is asked; first run is ≤3 steps, step 1 skippable; "Set as default home" uses the system prompt, once, at the right moment; no permission is requested during first run.** *Reconciliation flagged:* tracks 02/05 record that Pixel has no launcher first-run wizard at all — the first-run experience is the OS setup flow and the launcher inherits its result, and AOSP Launcher3 has no first-run tutorial overlay. Track 04 allows ≤3 steps for a third-party launcher. Marlow follows the stricter reading: show the populated home screen first, then at most the single system default-home prompt (see §4.4); there is no multi-screen wizard, no grid-size picker, no theme picker, no toggle questionnaire in first run — defaults are shown, not interrogated. After a decline, Marlow never re-prompts on launch; Settings contains a plain row ("Home app: not Marlow — Change in system settings") that opens Settings → Apps → Default apps → Home app. No account, no sign-in, no email, no phone number — ever, in first run. No "rate us", share prompt, or newsletter in the first session. First-run copy is one idea per screen, present tense, sentence case, no jargon. Check: fresh install → launch; count first-run screens and permission dialogs (zero before the home screen is usable). Source: Axo Launcher, "How to Change Your Default Launcher on Android" (system prompt since Android 10; "Don't ask again" behaviour); onboarding synthesis (dbggr.dev: value first; tours help only for complex products; only 1.2% of users changed a default in the cited 2005 study); Creahive onboarding guide; developer.android.com, Request runtime permissions. (T4 O1–O5, O9–O11; T2 §1.10; T5 §10.2.)

**67. Every later permission ask is primed, contextual, and cancellable; a declined permission degrades one feature, never the app.** A one-sentence priming screen states what the user gets; the user can cancel it without the system dialog ever firing; the system dialog fires only after opt-in on the priming screen. Deny a permission and everything except the dependent feature works, with no blocking error, no repeated re-prompt, and a one-line explanation on the dependent toggle. (Notifications: only when the user turns on a feature that needs it; contacts: only when the user adds a contacts shortcut/folder; notification-listener / accessibility / device-admin: only when the user enables that exact feature, via the system Settings screen Android requires; storage/photos: only when the user opens the wallpaper picker and chooses "your photos" — bundled wallpapers need no permission.) Check: deny each permission Marlow can request; use home, drawer, search, and settings. Source: developer.android.com, Request runtime permissions ("Ask for a permission in context, when the user starts to interact with the feature that requires it"; "gracefully degrade"); onboarding synthesis (a stated reason raises grant odds ~2.7×, asking in context ~1.5×, in the controlled study cited there). (T4 O6–O8.)

### J. Accessibility, large screens, performance, robustness

**68. Every interactive element has a ≥48 × 48dp touch target, including visually small icons (hit area expanded, icon not enlarged), with ≥8dp between adjacent targets.** System icons are 24dp (20dp for secondary icons). Screen margins are 16dp on phones (compact breakpoint) and 24dp on medium/expanded widths; all spacing snaps to the 4dp/8dp grid. The top 25% of large screens holds glanceable content, not primary controls. Layout changes at breakpoints: search is full-screen on phones and docked on tablets/foldables; dialogs are full-screen only on compact; a second pane appears only at expanded widths. Check: measure targets with layout inspection; measure margins on phone and tablet/foldable. Source: developer.android.com, Accessibility (48×48dp minimum); m3.material.io, State layers (interactive target 48dp); Breakpoints (compact <600dp, medium 600–839dp, expanded 840–1199dp; 16dp/24dp margins); M3 accessibility guidance (8dp between targets). (T1 R40–R46; T4 A1, A2.)

**69. TalkBack structure: the home screen is a list, not a picture.** Every icon-only control has a `contentDescription` naming its *action* ("Settings", not "gear icon"); app icons announce app name + position ("Chrome, page 1, position 3") and, where relevant, badge count; folders announce name + count ("Social, folder, 6 apps"); the drawer search field has a hint, not a contentDescription; state is exposed via `stateDescription` (selected / checked / expanded), not by colour; decorative elements (wallpaper art, decorative dividers, page-indicator decoration) are hidden from the accessibility tree. Every swipe/drag-only action has a `customActions` equivalent (move / remove / rename / uninstall), because TalkBack cannot swipe. Focus order is logical (left-to-right, top-to-bottom; home pages before dock; drawer list in reading order); focus enters a popup/dialog on open and returns to the invoking element on dismiss. Meaning is never conveyed by colour alone (a selected page dot needs shape/position as well as colour). Use native Compose controls wherever possible — they carry role and state for free. Check: navigate the whole launcher with TalkBack on. Source: developer.android.com, Accessibility; Compose accessibility guidance (droidsonroids guide, part 2). (T4 A7–A12.)

**70. Layout is a function of window size class, not device type — and fold/unfold reflows in place without losing state.** Grid columns, drawer columns, dock, and widget placement derive from the current window size class (Compact <600 dp, Medium 600–840 dp, Expanded ≥840 dp, plus Large/XLarge where applicable) via WindowManager metrics — never from "is tablet" / "is foldable" / a fixed aspect ratio. Unfolding or folding re-lays out the home screen in the same activity: same page, same widget positions, no restart, no lost scroll/draft state. Orientation comes from the occupied area, not the device (the original Pixel Fold opens in landscape; rotation_0 = landscape). On expanded widths the launcher and its settings adjust to the larger width (wider grid, two-pane settings where appropriate) rather than stretching the phone layout. Check: run in split-screen at three widths; unfold mid-scroll in the drawer and mid-drag of a widget — both survive. Source: developer.android.com, Support different display sizes ("Avoid using physical hardware values for making layout decisions"); Trifolds and landscape foldables doc; I/O 2024 large-screens recap (Fahd Imtiaz); Lawnchair 14 Beta 3 ("Proper tablet support"). (T6 R24–R27; T4 X11, T7–T8.)

**71. Marlow draws edge-to-edge and respects insets; the home screen is the predictive-back preview.** Content draws behind the status and navigation bars; tappable content is inset away from system bars; the wallpaper itself is not inset. From an app, an edge-swipe back slowly shows the actual home screen (wallpaper, icons, dock) shrinking into view — not a blank surface or placeholder. Never place a tap target fully inside the system gesture inset zones (screen edges for back, bottom for home). One-handed: dock, drawer handle, search, and back are all reachable/operable with one thumb. Check: screenshot with gesture navigation on; no label or button sits under a system bar. Source: Android Dev Summit 2019 (Banes & Shah, "Going Edge-to-Edge with Gesture Navigation"); developer.android.com, Ensure compatibility with gesture navigation; Predictive back design. (T6 R18; T4 A15, X12.)

**72. Performance, state correctness, and OEM robustness are quality bars, not afterthoughts.** Home screen is interactive in under ~1 s from a cold start on a mid-range device; page swipes and drawer pulls do not drop frames in a screen recording. State bugs are tested, not assumed away: search state clears when the drawer hides; bottom sheets reflect current state on open; customising an app that is being uninstalled does not crash; icon scaling on one surface does not leak to another; folder padding and the selected icon pack survive restarts. App-closing animations have a kill-switch (OEM gesture glitches, notably One UI, must be escapable without uninstalling). Marlow is exercised against the known launcher-hostile skins (One UI, ColorOS at minimum) for drawer, widget add, and settings-with-wallpaper-preview paths. Backup/restore restores the full layout (home, dock, folders, widgets, icon pack) on a new device — Pixel's absence of this is a known gap Marlow must not repeat. Nova-backup import works (home/dock grid, widgets/folders, selected icon pack, per the Lawnchair 15 Beta 3 scope) or Marlow documents precisely what it does not import. Check: script each scenario; run the smoke set on each skin. Source: T2 §6.4, rule 42, §2.1 (backup); Lawnchair 15 Beta 2 / 2.1 / Beta 3 changelogs; Microsoft Launcher 6.2 changelog (folder padding, icon-pack persistence); Smart Launcher 3 ("widget picker is now much faster"). (T2 rules 41, 42; T6 R5, R30, R33–R35, R32.)

## 4. The Marlow design standard

This section applies §3 to Marlow's locked decisions. Where a locked decision goes beyond what the research prescribes, that is stated as a project decision, not dressed up as a research finding.

### 4.1 Stock theme, P22 accent, warm shading, halo

**Stock theme (rule 4, 5).** The Stock theme is a static M3 scheme generated from the single P22-blue seed through the HCT pipeline (Material Theme Builder or `material-color-utilities`), with both light and dark schemes exported. No role is hand-tuned afterwards. Material You is the same pipeline with the wallpaper as the seed — one tap away in Settings/the wizard — and the app swaps the *scheme*, never individual colours. Dynamic colour is on by default on Android 12+; the Stock scheme is the fallback below API 31 and when dynamic colour is off (rule 3).

**P22 accent (rules 1, 2, 4).** P22 phosphor blue (`#3333CC` light / `#5555FF` dark) is the *seed*, not a colour applied directly to UI elements. Every accent in the UI is a role derived from that seed (`primary`, `primary-container`, `secondary-container`, and so on), paired only with its matching `on-` role. The accent must survive all three user contrast levels (standard/medium/high); if a pairing breaks at high contrast, the fix is in the derivation, never a one-off darkening of text. The default scheme variant is `tonal-spot` (the Android Material You variant); a "vibrant" or "expressive" option in theme settings would be a different variant of the same engine, not a different engine.

**Warm CRT shading (rules 4, 6, 17).** The warm light background (`#F8F1E7`) and dark background (`#262019`) are choices about the *neutral* tones of the scheme, expressed through `surface` / `surface-container-*` roles, so every component that reads a surface role inherits the warmth consistently. Depth comes from those tonal steps, not from shadows — the warm family must still read as a depth system in dark theme, where shadows are invisible. The warmth is compatible with M3 Expressive's direction (bolder, more emotional interfaces that users complete tasks faster in — 46 studies, 18,000+ participants, key elements spotted up to 4× faster); a timid, grey, flat home screen would read as dated against Expressive, while the warm phosphor identity is an asset in that frame.

**Halo (rule 8).** The soft subtle halo appears **only** on the page-indicator dot and the widget resize handle — the two locked placements. It is drawn *in addition to* the role system (a glow around a `primary`-coloured dot, a glow around the resize handle), never as a substitute for a role, and never the only indicator of state: turn the halo off and every state must still read (the dot still reads as selected by position/shape/colour role; the resize handle still reads as a handle by shape and placement). The halo carries no information that colour alone carries, and it appears nowhere else — not on icons, not on the dock, not on the search bar, not on folders. **[APP]** — M3 has no halo rule; these constraints are the application of M3's state-layer and colour-role rules to a brand effect.

**Icon C v3 (rules 45–48).** Icon C v3 must pass rule 45 as a proper adaptive icon (108×108 dp layers, mark 48–66 dp in the 66×66 dp safe zone, monochrome silhouette layer, no baked-in mask/shadow/text) and rule 46 (survives every mask at 48 px). Its monochrome layer must be tested against warm, cool, desaturated, and neutral tints on light and dark backgrounds (rule 48).

### 4.2 The default home — first-boot composition

| Zone | Contents (default) | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Top | Glance strip: day + date and weather (condition + temperature), no card, no border, text directly on wallpaper; an upcoming event replaces the date line | 27 |
| Upper-middle | At most one widget besides the strip, and it must not duplicate the strip (clock *or* weather, not both, never a duplicate of At a Glance) | 21 |
| Middle | 8–12 app/folder cells, arranged by action/frequency (not alphabetically, not by colour), with at least one full row left empty | 20, 21 |

| Bottom edge, below dock | One search pill, on every page | 28 |

**What page one must not carry on first boot:** a second page of apps the user never asked for, an empty page, a tips card, a settings shortcut, a store shortcut, more than two information widgets, or any promotional element (rules 20, 21). **Grid:** 5×5 default (4×5 on smaller phones); grid options exactly 2×2 / 3×3 / 4×4 / 4×5 / 5×5 (rule 22). **Labels:** 12sp, one role, one line + ellipsis, on the grid; off in the dock (rules 10, 11, 25). **Legibility:** luminance-aware scrim under the label/glance zones, strong over bright art, fading to nothing over dark art, no visible edge (rule 14). **Wallpaper:** parallaxes with page scroll (rule 29). **Badges:** dot only, top-right, one colour, cleared on open (rules 49, 50). **Folders on first boot:** at most two, each with a short task-based name (e.g. "Google"); no folder named "Folder" (rule 39). **New apps:** go to the drawer, not page one (rule 30).

### 4.4 First run — what Marlow must ask

1. **Nothing before value.** The populated home screen (§4.2) appears first. No wizard, no permission dialog, no sign-in, no theme/grid questionnaire before it.
2. **One question: "Set as default home" — via Android's own system prompt, once, at the right moment.** Marlow triggers the system "Set Marlow as your default home app?" prompt; the user taps "Set as default" and the home screen opens. The prompt is explained in one line, then Marlow gets out of the way.
3. **Done — home screen.** At most three first-run screens in total, each skippable, and completing or skipping lands on the home screen.

After a decline, Marlow never re-prompts on launch (including after "Don't ask again" is ticked); Settings carries the plain fallback row to Settings → Apps → Default apps → Home app (rule 66). Every permission is deferred and contextual (rule 67). No account, no sign-in, no "rate us", no share prompt, no newsletter in first run.

## 5. Top 20 rules

The twenty that will matter most when the rebuild is judged — the ones a user notices in the first five seconds, and the ones whose absence reads as "not stock" rather than "missing a feature".

1. **Rule 20 — First boot is not empty.** A curated page 0 + dock + glanceable strip on fresh install. An empty home is the single biggest stock-feel failure.
2. **Rule 2 — Pair every colour with its `on-` role.** Broken pairings are the fastest way to look wrong: illegible text, buttons that fail at high contrast, a theme that falls apart the moment the wallpaper changes.
3. **Rule 3 — Dynamic colour on, Stock scheme as the fallback.** A Pixel launcher that doesn't theme from the wallpaper isn't a Pixel launcher; a launcher with no coherent static fallback isn't finished.
4. **Rule 13 — Labels meet 4.5:1 against the worst patch behind them.** If the labels are unreadable on the user's photo, nothing else matters.
5. **Rule 10 — One 12sp label style for all icons.** Mixed label sizes are the most visible amateur tell on a home screen.
6. **Rule 24 — One icon size, one label size, one gutter — everywhere.** Home, drawer, folders, dock, and search results must measure identically.
7. **Rule 25 — Five-icon dock, the daily five by kind, labels off, on every page.** The dock is the anchor of the whole screen; five stable, familiar apps, never changing.
8. **Rule 27 — At a Glance is a living strip, not a widget.** No card, no border; date/weather resting state; a calendar event replaces the date line; swiping cycles real complications.
9. **Rule 32 — The drawer is alphabetical, vertical, and complete — and search is device-first.** No custom order, every app exactly once, typing filters instantly.
10. **Rule 49 — Dots mean presence, never a number; long-press is notifications, then shortcuts, then App info.** A count badge is the Nova tell; a missing shortcut row is the amateur tell.
11. **Rule 48 — Themed icons from the monochrome layer only, never forced tinting.** Themed icons are where Material You either looks unified or looks muddy; the difference is a real silhouette layer versus a colour filter. (And the all-or-nothing question in rule 48 is still open — see §7.)
12. **Rule 46 — One mask for every app icon.** Mixed masks are the fastest visual tell of an amateur launcher.
13. **Rule 45 — Marlow's own icon is a proper adaptive icon that survives every mask at 48 px.** The launcher's own icon is the first icon the user sees; if it clips under a circular mask, the launcher looks broken before it is even opened.
14. **Rule 37 — Folders are drag-to-create, circular 2×2 preview, overlay-open, named at the bottom, never nested, drop-gated.** Any other folder behaviour is immediately non-stock.
15. **Rule 15 — Shape discipline (pills, 12dp cards, 28dp sheets/dialogs).** Shape is how the user tells a button from a card from a sheet at a glance.
16. **Rule 17 — Tonal elevation, not shadows.** Heavy drop shadows are the clearest Material 2 tell.
17. **Rule 19 — Press feedback on everything, from the touch point.** A home screen with dead-feeling icons feels broken even when it works.
18. **Rule 53 — Emphasized easing, exits faster than enters (400 ms in, 200 ms out).** Motion is where "stock" is felt rather than seen.
19. **Rule 56 — Long-press at the system 500 ms, reorder after a 650 ms hover.** Timing is platform muscle memory, not a tuning knob; the deliberate pause is a large part of why stock dragging feels calm.
20. **Rule 57 — Do not fight the system gesture.** Marlow does not own Home or Recents; it owns the workspace swipe-up to the drawer. The swipe-home animation is icon-anchored where the platform allows it, honestly recorded on the Pixel 10a — pretending it is fixed when it isn't is exactly how a launcher ends up feeling wrong on the device while looking fine in a build log.

## 7. Gaps and open questions

What the research could not settle and must be decided on the device or by judgement. Nothing in this section is a rule; each item needs a device check or an explicit Marlow decision before it is built on.

1. **Factory-default dock contents on a clean Pixel first boot.** Google publishes no canonical list; screenshot evidence and fork claims conflict (5 vs 6 items). Verify on a real Pixel before hard-coding any list as "the Pixel default". Marlow's dock (§4.2) is Marlow's curated choice by kind.
2. **Exact first-boot Pixel app set.** The same caution applies to which apps (if any) are pre-placed on page 0 beyond At a Glance, and to the current `default_workspace_5x5.xml` contents on Pixel — AOSP's default and Pixel's shipped default differ.
3. **Themed icons: ship the mixed grid, or refuse themed icons until forced theming?** Tracks disagree (rule 48). Below Android 16 QPR2, offering themed icons means a mixed grid (documented behaviour); refusing them means declining a system feature on principle (the Niagara position). This is an explicit Marlow decision, not settled by the research.
4. **Badge model: dot-only, or numeric badges as a non-stock extension?** M3 defines a count badge; Pixel ships a dot; Launcher3's code draws a number when count > 0. Marlow's position (rule 49) is dot-only on stock surfaces, but the numeric-badge question — and the badge-size conflict (M3 6dp dot / 16dp count vs Launcher3 older dimens 24dp / 12dp text) — needs a device measurement against the shipping Pixel build.
5. **Exact current `REORDER_TIMEOUT` on the shipping Pixel build** (650 ms in current AOSP main; 350 ms in older trees). Confirm which the Pixel build carries; Marlow uses 650 ms meanwhile.
6. **Exact `badge_size` / `badge_text_size` on current Pixel dimens** (24dp / 12dp is the older value; the ratio, not the absolute, is the invariant).
7. **Whether the shipping Pixel drawer still uses the ragged section-merge algorithm** or has moved to strict grid sections (rule 32).
8. **The precise fallback rendering for themed icons of apps without a monochrome layer** on the current release.
9. **"Show long app names" / nine-character trim** — reported from an Android 15 DP2 teardown, not Google-documented; treat the trim as observed behaviour and the two-line fix as reported direction (rule 11).
10. **At a Glance removal toggle** — spotted in a Canary build, non-functional when spotted; do not design around it shipping. Related: whether At a Glance can ever be removed, and what its empty/off state must show, needs a device check.
11. **"Tiered widgets"** — a flag spotted in Android 16 DP code suggesting the picker may show only a few widgets with a "Show all" button; no screenshots, no shipped behaviour at time of writing.
12. **Suggestions-row toggle: can it be turned off, and where does it live?** Sources disagree and the location drifted across releases (rule 34). Marlow's one-toggle position is decided; Pixel's current state needs a device check.
13. **Widget stacking as a Marlow extension.** AOSP has no stacking; iOS proves the idea. If Marlow ships it, the same-size-only rule, the affordance, auto-rotate behaviour, and the reset-on-Home rule all need device testing (rule 44).
14. **Drawer folders / categorised drawer view.** Pixel has no folders in the drawer; Lawnchair added them as its most-requested feature; the iOS study suggests a category view. Whether Marlow adds either — and whether that dilutes the "alphabetical, complete" drawer (rule 32) — is an explicit Marlow decision.
15. **The November 2025 dock-search reroute.** The home-screen bar now opens the Google app (AI Mode) while drawer-top search remains the device-first surface. Which surface Marlow's dock pill opens — web-first, device-first, or a Marlow unified search — and whether the two surfaces stay distinct, is a Marlow decision the research frames but does not settle (rules 28, 33, 35).
16. **Icon sizes per grid** (~57 dp for 5×5, ~66 dp for 4×4/4×5, ~89 dp for 3×3) are fork/secondary evidence, indicative not AOSP-canonical — verify by measurement.
17. **Nova's current status** — sources disagree by date (effectively unmaintained after Sept 2025 vs acquired by Instabridge and updating again in 2026). Verify before citing Nova as a live product; its design lessons stand on the changelogs themselves.
18. **I/O 2026 / Android 17 / Remote Compose host-side requirements** are very recent (May–Oct 2026); verify the exact host-side requirements against the current Glance/Remote Compose docs before implementing. Treat Remote Compose capabilities as the direction of travel for host support, not as APIs Marlow must adopt on day one.
19. **No Google talk is about building a launcher.** Launcher guidance was assembled from widget, gesture, Material You, and large-screen talks/docs. Any "launcher talk" claim beyond the sessions listed in §6 would be invented. Exact YouTube video IDs were not verified for most I/O talks, so none are given as links.
20. **Niagara's primary changelog site was reached via a forum mirror (4PDA);** the substance (Material You backport, themed-icon refusal, rounded widget corners, approximate location) is quoted from the team's own post as reproduced there. Microsoft Launcher has no single design blog; its section rests on Garage-era changelogs as reported by the press.
21. **The Marlow settings map (§4.3) and the first-boot counts (§4.2) are proposals to test, not Pixel facts.** The map's example grid subtext ("5 × 4") conflicts with the 5×5 default and must show the actual current grid. Both need approval and device testing before they are treated as settled.
